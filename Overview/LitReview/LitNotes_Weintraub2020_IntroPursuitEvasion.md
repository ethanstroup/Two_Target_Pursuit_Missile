# Weintraub, Pachter, & Garcia, 2020 — An Introduction to Pursuit-Evasion Differential Games

## Citation
Weintraub, I. E., Pachter, M., & Garcia, E. (2020). "An Introduction to Pursuit-evasion Differential Games." *arXiv:2003.05013 [math.OC]*.

## Problem Framing
- **Game type:** Survey/tutorial, not a single novel result. Structured as (1) historical overview of differential games, (2) literature categorization by player count, (3) two fully worked closed-form case studies. The two case studies span both **game-of-kind** (win/lose partition, Sec. V-C) and **game-of-degree** (optimal strategy synthesis given an outcome, Sec. IV, V-D).
- **Agent structure:** Case Study 1 (Sec. IV, "Two Cutters and Fugitive Ship"): 2 heterogeneous-speed pursuers vs. 1 evader, holonomic simple motion, $\beta_1,\beta_2>1$. Case Study 2 (Sec. V, Active Target Defense): 3 agents — Attacker, Target, Defender — with Target+Defender cooperating against Attacker, $V_A=V_D$, $\alpha=V_T/V_A<1$.
- **Dimensionality:** Both case studies are 2D planar, simple-motion (instantaneous heading control, no turn-rate constraint). Sec. IV state space $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^6$; Sec. V reduced state space $(x_A,x_T,y_T)\in\mathbb{R}^3$ via symmetry.

## Core Contribution
No single new theorem — the paper's value is a **complete, mechanically transparent worked example of the Isaacs Main-Equation machinery on a multi-pursuer problem with an explicit simultaneity condition**, plus a full HJI-verification proof (Theorem 2) that Garcia et al. (2017), the source paper for Sec. IV, only outlines.

Central result (Sec. IV, Two-Pursuer-One-Evader, "2P1EDG"): the state space partitions into $\mathcal{R}_1,\mathcal{R}_2,\mathcal{R}_s$ (captured by $P_1$ only / $P_2$ only / **simultaneously by both**). In $\mathcal{R}_s$, the optimal strategy is: find the intersection of the two players' **Apollonius circles** farthest from the evader's position — the **aimpoint** $I$ — and have all three agents head straight at it, guaranteeing simultaneous arrival. The Value function (capture time) is proven $C^1$ across all three regions and shown to satisfy the HJI PDE everywhere, including at region boundaries — the load-bearing algebraic step being a *reverse-engineered convex-combination weight* $w=-F_2/(F_1-F_2)$ chosen specifically so that the value function's sensitivity to the (analytically intractable) implicit aimpoint direction $\phi^*(\mathbf{x})$ vanishes identically, sidestepping the need to ever differentiate through $d\phi^*/d\mathbf{x}$.

## Method
- **Dynamic programming / HJI PDE**, in the Hespanha state-feedback verification-theorem form: $V(t,\mathbf{x})$ costate $\lambda:=\partial V/\partial\mathbf{x}$ Hamiltonian $H=\lambda\cdot f+g$ $\min\max H$ over controls gives optimal headings as normalized-costate directions ($\cos\phi^*=\lambda_{x}/\|\lambda\|$-type formulas). Simple motion ($f$ has no $\mathbf{x}$-dependence) $\Rightarrow$ $\dot\lambda=-\partial H/\partial\mathbf{x}=0$ $\Rightarrow$ costates constant $\Rightarrow$ optimal trajectories are straight lines.
- **Apollonius-circle intersection** as the geometric embodiment of the $t_{f1}=t_{f2}$ simultaneity constraint: each circle is the locus of points reachable by the evader at the same instant a given pursuer could reach them; the farthest mutual intersection point is the unique aimpoint satisfying both single-pursuer tie conditions at once.
- **Sec. V** instead uses the **two-sided Pontryagin Minimum Principle** with Lagrange-multiplier transversality conditions ($\lambda(t_f)=-\partial\Phi_a/\partial\mathbf{x}_f$) to pin down a reduced-state TPBVP, reducing to a scalar optimization over a quartic equation (49) — a genuinely different bookkeeping route to the same class of answer as the dynamic-programming route in Sec. IV.

## Key Terminology Introduced
- **Aimpoint** $I=(x_I,y_I)$: the farther of two Apollonius-circle intersection points; all cooperating agents head directly at it to guarantee simultaneous arrival.
- **Dispersal surface** $\mathcal{D}$: singular surface where the two Apollonius-circle intersections are equidistant from the evader — two optimal aimpoints exist simultaneously, and each side's independent choice between them determines whether the game stays on or leaves the singular surface (Isaacs' "perpetual dilemma" if both always match).
- **Regions $\mathcal{R}_1,\mathcal{R}_2,\mathcal{R}_s$**: state-space partition by which pursuer(s) capture the evader under optimal play; boundaries characterized by equality in conditions (18)/(19).
- **MBMC-style team assignment** (Sec. IV-D, 5v3 extension): using the 2P1E value function as a building block, assign pursuer pairs/singles to evaders to minimize worst-case capture time — combinatorial layer distinct from the geometric solution itself.

## Connections to Prior Literature
- **Isaacs' *Differential Games*:** This paper is a direct, notation-modernized walkthrough of Isaacs' own dynamic-programming/tenet-of-transition derivation of the ME, using Hespanha's verification-theorem framing instead of Isaacs' original notation. The costate variable $\lambda=\partial V/\partial\mathbf{x}$ and the ME $\min\max H\equiv0$ map directly onto the ME₁/ME₂ apparatus already reviewed in Isaacs Ch. 4. Sec. IV-D's dispersal surface is a concrete worked instance of Isaacs' singular-surface machinery (dispersal is one of the named types alongside barrier, universal, equivocal).
- **Yan et al. (2022):** Both solve a multi-agent coupling constraint via convex geometry, but by structurally different routes. Yan's KKT/HJI equivalence reformulates the value function as a convex program; this paper's $\mathcal{R}_s$ value function reverse-engineers a *convex-combination weight* between two competing single-pursuer cost formulas so that sensitivity to an intractable implicit variable ($\phi^*$) cancels by construction. Both are "avoid differentiating through the hard part" tricks, but target different hard parts (Yan: the HJI PDE itself; here: an implicit aimpoint direction).
- **Mammadov & Ranasinghe:** Sharp contrast — M&R's area-gradient law is instantaneous/greedy with literal Apollonius **discs** (regions) and no terminal condition beyond $A_e\to0$; this paper uses Apollonius **circles** (boundaries only) as a *terminal simultaneity locus*, solved once in closed form via full-horizon straight-line optimality, not recomputed each instant. Same primitive construct, opposite purpose.
- **Dillon et al.:** This paper's constant-costate/straight-line result is the simple-motion case that Dillon et al.'s Dubins-style heading-rate dynamics explicitly break — $\partial H/\partial\mathbf{x}\ne0$ once positional structure (WEZ geometry, turn-rate constraints) enters the Hamiltonian, at which point costates stop being constant and this whole closed-form apparatus fails, forcing direct collocation. This paper is the clean baseline case; Dillon et al. is the concrete evidence of where it breaks.
- **My 2v1 synchronized-impact problem:** Sec. IV's $t_{f1}=t_{f2}$ condition is the closest structural match found so far to my terminal simultaneity constraint — same equality-coupling form, just with pursuer/evader roles inverted relative to my friendlies/target setup. The aimpoint-via-circle-intersection technique is a candidate closed-form route *if* my target is non-adversarial or has sufficiently simple (point-capture, non-WEZ) terminal geometry; it is a third named technique for simultaneity constraints alongside Yan's KKT/HJI route and Dillon's direct-collocation fallback.

## Limitations / Open Questions
- Sec. IV's closed-form aimpoint solution depends entirely on **simple motion** (no turn-rate constraint) and **point capture** — neither holds once WEZ/cardioid engagement geometry or Dubins-style dynamics enter, which is very likely for my problem.
- The evader in Sec. IV is a single, non-cooperating, non-WEZ target — no aspect-angle or engagement-envelope structure at all; direct transfer to a WEZ-defended bandit is unproven.
- Dispersal-surface handling (Sec. IV-D) assumes each side "pays only an infinitesimally small price" for miscoordinated tie-breaking — this is asserted from the worked example, not proven in general.
- Sec. V's ATDDG is a defense scenario (2 cooperate to intercept 1 attacker before it reaches a target), not an offensive synchronized-strike scenario — geometry is structurally different from my problem despite superficial 3-agent similarity.
- Survey citations (Hagedorn & Breakwell; Hayoun & Shima [61], noted as solving **bounded-control, equal-intercept-time** 2v1 pursuit) are flagged but not detailed in-text — worth chasing directly, as Hayoun & Shima appears to address explicit synchronization more directly than this paper does.

## Relevance to My Research
The Sec. IV aimpoint/Apollonius-intersection technique is the most mechanically concrete simultaneity-constraint solution reviewed so far — a genuine third alternative to Yan et al.'s KKT/HJI route and Dillon et al.'s direct-collocation fallback, and cheap (closed-form, no PDE or NLP solve) when its simple-motion/point-capture assumptions hold. Its likely failure mode for my problem is exactly where Dillon et al.'s numerical approach becomes necessary: once WEZ-style terminal geometry or non-holonomic dynamics are added, costates stop being constant and the clean geometric aimpoint construction no longer applies.
