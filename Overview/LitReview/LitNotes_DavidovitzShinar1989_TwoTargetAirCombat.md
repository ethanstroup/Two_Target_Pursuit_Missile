# Davidovitz & Shinar, 1989 — Two-Target Game Model of Air Combat with Fire-and-Forget All-Aspect Missiles

## Citation
Davidovitz, A., and Shinar, J., "Two-Target Game Model of an Air Combat with Fire-and-Forget All-Aspect Missiles," *Journal of Optimization Theory and Applications*, Vol. 63, No. 2, Nov. 1989, pp. 133–165.

## Problem Framing
- **Game type:** Pure game of kind (win/lose/mutual-kill/draw state-space partition; no value function or cost functional — game of degree explicitly out of scope). Solves via a systematic synthesis method (Ref. 17, Shinar & Davidovitz 1987): decompose into **two independent single-target pursuit-evasion games of kind**, then combine via explicit rules (Appendix B).
- **Agent structure:** 2 players, 1v1, fully symmetric/homogeneous — "game of two identical cars" dynamics (Merz, 1972): both turn-rate-limited to the same bound, unit speed, both carry **identical all-aspect fire-and-forget missiles** (genuine bidirectional lethality — each player owns a target set $T_i$ representing his own weapon envelope against the other).
- **Dimensionality:** Reduced state space $(R,\phi_1,\phi_2)\in\mathbb{R}\times S^1\times S^1$ (3D) via LOS-relative coordinates — range plus one off-boresight angle per player, both dynamic since both players are turn-constrained (vs. only one angle-state in Getz & Pachter, where player 2's heading was direct control).

## Core Contribution
Two linked results. (1) **No-escape WEZ target-set model**: aspect-angle-dependent min/max firing range ($\underline R_i(\phi_j)=a+b\cos\phi_j$, $\bar R_i(\phi_j)=\bar R_0-|\phi_j+\sin\phi_j|$), derived assuming the target flies its own optimal evasive maneuver (Appendix A) — a cardioid-like envelope, but justified by an explicit evasion argument rather than asserted. (2) **Systematic two-target synthesis** (Appendix B): three cases based on how $(UP)_i$ relates to $T_j$/$(UP)_j$; Case III (the interesting one) gives mutual kill as $W_{1,2}=M'\cup M$, $M'=(W_p)_1'\cap(W_p)_2'$ (direct overlap of *corrected* zones), $M=[(W_p)_1\cap(W_p)_2]\setminus[(W_p)_1'\cup(W_p)_2']$ (naive-zone overlap excluded from both corrected zones by target-set modification). Worked numerical example ($\beta=45°$, $\bar R_0=6.14$, $a=0.55$, $b=0.3$) finds $(W_p)_1'\cap(W_p)_2'=\phi$ (Eq. 87) — so $M'=\phi$ and mutual kill reduces to **pure $M$**, which turns out to be only **four measure-zero semipermeable surfaces** ($|\phi_1|=|\phi_2|$), not an open volume. Each player's winning zone is **five disconnected closed subregions** (two small max-range/head-on zones, two off-boresight-limit zones, one large minimum-range zone governed by angular advantage $|\phi_i|<|\phi_j|$). The dominant min-range barrier strategies reproduce real air-combat **scissors maneuvers**.

## Method
Isaacs' BUP/semipermeable-surface machinery, executed via **costate/transversality formalism** ($\dot\lambda=-\partial H/\partial x$, $\lambda_f=n_i$) rather than direct curve-ODE integration — necessary because the barrier here is a genuine 2D surface in 3D state space, not a single graph $y(x)$. A conserved first integral $(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)^2/R^2+\lambda_R^2=1$ (verified symbolically to hold unconditionally along the coupled state–costate system, independent of control choice) enables closed-form integration of piecewise-constant-control segments. Each single-target game is solved **twice** per player — once with the original $T_i$, once with a modified $T_i'\triangleq T_i\setminus\bar T_j$ — because Rule III's mutual-kill computation needs both. Step 4 (closedness/non-leaking test) is case-by-case, not formulaic, and generates a large singular-surface taxonomy (Table 3: universal, dispersal, semidispersal, switch, switch-trajectory, equivocal, and commuting lines) requiring individual certification, analogous in role to Getz & Pachter's Lyapunov certification but resolving surface topology rather than region invariance.

## Key Terminology Introduced
- **No-escape range $R_{NE}$**: WEZ boundary derived assuming the target executes its own optimal evasive maneuver — not a static/non-reactive envelope.
- **Modified target/usable part** ($T_i'\triangleq T_i\setminus\bar T_j$, $(UP)_i'\triangleq(UP)_i\setminus\bar T_j$): corrected accounting removing territory already preempted by the opponent's own target.
- **Mutual-kill decomposition** ($M'$ vs. $M$): $M'$ = direct overlap of corrected winning zones (unambiguous mutual kill); $M$ = naive-zone overlap that correction excludes from *both* players' final zones (contested-by-elimination).
- **Stagnation point**: state where $\dot R=\dot\phi_1=\dot\phi_2=0$ simultaneously.
- **Commuting line**: singular surface patching a "hole" in an otherwise-closed barrier, seeded at a stagnation point.
- **Semidispersal line / switch trajectory / equivocal line**: additional singular-surface types beyond the dispersal/universal pair, specific to this problem's richer barrier topology.

## Connections to Prior Literature
- **Getz & Pachter (1981):** This is the homogeneous, fully-turn-limited "game of two cars" case G&P's homicidal-chauffeur asymmetry stood in for (and pointed forward to, via Olsder & Breakwell / Merz & Hague). Same BUP/retrogression logic, but costate-based rather than G&P's direct curve-ODE — required by the extra state dimension. G&P's trichotomy never formalized mutual kill as a distinct outcome; this paper is the first reviewed source giving it a clean general definition ($T_1\cap T_2$) and a systematic rule-based synthesis (Appendix B), generalizing what G&P handled ad hoc via hand-picked Lyapunov certificates.
- **Weintraub, Pachter & Garcia (2020):** Same costate object $\lambda=\nabla V$ and transversality principle — here collapsing to the bare geometric form $\lambda_f=n_i$ since a game of kind has no terminal payoff, vs. their $\lambda(t_f)=-\partial\Phi_a/\partial x_f$. Same underlying Main-Equation apparatus, applied to a barrier (game of kind) instead of a value function (game of degree). Their dispersal-surface tie-break assumption parallels this paper's "prefer draw to mutual kill" convention and its richer singular-surface zoo.
- **Dillon, Zollars, Weintraub & Von Moll (2023):** Shares aspect-angle-dependent WEZ framing, but Dillon's WEZ is non-reactive (single-agent OCP); this paper's no-escape envelope is derived from the target's own optimal evasion, embedded in a genuine two-sided game — the true adversarial counterpart to Dillon's obstacle-avoidance framing.
- **Yan et al. (2022) / Mammadov & Ranasinghe (2025):** Both one-sided (pursuers capture, evader can't reciprocate); this paper (like Getz & Pachter) has genuine bidirectional lethality, requiring target-set union/intersection logic ($T=T_1\cup T_2$, $T_{1,2}=T_1\cap T_2$) with no analog in either.
- **My 2v1 problem:** The Appendix B synthesis recipe (decompose into single-target games, correct for target-set overlap, combine via explicit rules) is the most reusable *general algorithm* found so far for multi-target/bidirectional-lethality structure — not just a worked example. The $M'$/$M$ decomposition and the empirical collapse of mutual kill to a measure-zero surface is concrete evidence that "both die" may be the exception rather than the norm in a correctly-formalized bidirectional-lethality game, directly bearing on how to structure the terminal condition when one of my two attackers is lost.

## Limitations / Open Questions
- Pure game of kind — no value function/time-to-capture; game of degree explicitly unsolved (same gap as Getz & Pachter).
- 1v1 only, no team/cooperation structure — doesn't address synchronization, the core of my own problem.
- Optimal control law ($\sigma^*=-\text{sign}(\lambda)$) is only rigorously derived along barrier trajectories seeded from BUP points via transversality — **not** a global feedback law over winning-zone interiors. Directly relevant to any attempt to simulate "optimal play" from arbitrary initial conditions.
- Fully symmetric parameters and identical target sets throughout — doesn't test heterogeneous missile envelopes or speeds.
- The specific result ($M'=\phi$, mutual kill collapsing to measure-zero surfaces) is **parameter-dependent** ($\beta=45°$, $\bar R_0=6.14$, $a=0.55$, $b=0.3$); Rule III's general form permits genuine open-volume mutual kill. Don't over-generalize "mutual kill is fragile" beyond this specific regime.
- Step 4's closedness/non-leaking certification is case-by-case, not a general closed-form test — same unglamorous, load-bearing verification burden as Getz & Pachter's Lyapunov work.

## Relevance to My Research
The closest source yet to a genuine homogeneous, bidirectionally-lethal 1v1 mutual-kill base case — supplying both the WEZ-embedded-in-a-two-sided-game formalization Getz & Pachter lacked and a systematic, reusable synthesis algorithm (Appendix B) for combining single-target solutions that could plausibly generalize toward my own multi-target 2v1 structure. The finding that mutual kill collapsed to a measure-zero knife-edge in this parameter regime is a valuable existence proof that bidirectional lethality doesn't automatically imply a robust "both die" outcome region — worth testing whether an analogous collapse occurs once I formalize the homogeneous 2v1 case.
