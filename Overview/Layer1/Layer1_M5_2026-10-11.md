  - The derivation is in criteria §2. Check 1b confirms it numerically.

  - $201\times204^2$ and $101\times102^2$, as for M3 and M4.

- **Horizon.** $T_{RA}=6$, by the pre-registered rule.
  - The coarse grid was marched to $\tau=10$. Every 0.25-step after $\tau=6$ moves less than
    0.21 % of $\{V^{RA}_1=0\}$, and $\{V^{RA}_1\le0\}$ grows only from 8.110 % to 8.148 % of the
    grid between $\tau=6$ and 10.
  - The coarse fields at $\tau=6$ are cut from that march (`reach_avoid.py cut`).
- **$D$.** Uses M3's $V_1$ at $\tau=6$ on both main grids, with the same horizon, so no fallback
  was needed. The $51\times51^2$ grid has its own single-target solve.
- **Live states.** $\Omega$ is $R\ge0.45$ outside both target sets. It has 899,333 nodes on the
  coarse grid and 7,171,813 on the fine one.
- **Cost on the 2-core container.**
  - Fine solve: 16,569 s (4.6 h).
  - Coarse to $\tau=10$: 1,478 s.
  - The full acceptance run: about 9 min.

## 4. Findings

### 4.1 The guarantee holds

- **Check 11.** Player 1 flies $-\operatorname{sign}(\partial V^{RA}_1/\partial\phi_1)$, read at
  the remaining horizon. He wins alone from every start against each player-2 law: the
  reach-avoid adversary, avoid, trade, heuristic and random bang-bang. That is 11,895 runs on the
  fine grid and 10,110 on the coarse one.
- **The harness can register a loss.** This was checked separately (`diag_negctl_101x102.log`).
  From 400 deep starts against the trading player 2, a coasting player 1 wins only 47, and one
  turning away wins none.
- **The obstacle placement is right.** Checks 1b and 2 test it against exact solutions where those
  exist.

### 4.2 D&S's corrected game is reproduced

- **Table 2.** All 17 tagged points satisfy their criterion (check 10).
  - This includes $\alpha,\varepsilon,\rho_1,\zeta_1,\tau$, which exist only in the corrected
    construction: $|V^{RA}_1|=0.040,\ 0.022,\ 0.019,\ 0.017,\ 0.010$ against $\delta=0.059$.
  - Every off-target point is within $0.6\,\delta$.
- **The off-boresight zones.** They are two $Rf$-mirror components of 3757 nodes each, at
  $R\in[4.74,6.10]$, $|\phi_1|\in[46°,74°]$ and $|\phi_2|\le41°$. That is D&S's $\beta\rho_1\delta$
  triangles in Fig. 14, where the paper puts $\rho_1$ at $(5.686,\ 77.03°,\ -6.86°)$.
- **The mutual-kill surfaces.**
  - All 3608 nodes in $W_1\cap W_2$ lie **exactly** on $|\phi_1|=|\phi_2|$, with
    $|\phi_1|\in[45.9°,130.6°]$ and $|V^{RA}_1|<0.1\,\delta$.
  - This is D&S Eq. (89): four surfaces $|\phi_1|=|\phi_2|>\beta$ where the two winning zones touch.
  - In the continuum $V^{RA}_1=0$ there. On the coarse grid those nodes fall on the positive side,
    and on the fine grid on the negative side. That sign noise is why check 6 sees no overlap at
    101 and 3608 nodes at 201.
- **D&S's open mutual-kill volume $M$ is empty, as they found.** $M$ is "both can force a kill,
  but neither corrected zone": $(\{V_1\le0\}\cap\{V_2\le0\})\setminus(W_1\cup W_2)$. It is
  0.002 % of $\Omega$.

### 4.3 The contested set is fat, and it is the old set's over-claim

**What it is.** On the fine grid $C$ has 75,752 nodes.
- 49.9 % have $V_1\le0<V_2$: player 1 can force a kill against an avoiding player 2, and player 2
  cannot do the same.
- 49.9 % are the mirror case.
- 0.2 % have both.

The player-1 half is **exactly** M3's old "win-alone" set minus $W_1$: 100 % of that difference
lies in $C$. The 2026-10-01 review said this set over-claimed. This measures by how much: 0.53 % of
$\Omega$, about 14 % of the old set.

**Where it is.** Two $Rf$-mirror volumes of 18,841 nodes each:
- $R\in[3.2,5.7]$, with a median of 4.45;
- $|\phi_1|\in[46°,111°]$, so player 1 is off his own boresight;
- $\phi_2$ spans $[-78°,44°]$ with a mean of $-13°$ when $\phi_1>0$ (the mirror when
  $\phi_1<0$), so player 2 is pointing roughly at player 1.

Player 1 can force a kill there in the single-target game ($V_1$ median $-2.8\,\delta$). But
bringing his nose around takes him through player 2's envelope, so player 2 can always force the
trade. Player 1 cannot win alone ($V^{RA}_1$ median $+3.9\,\delta$), and player 2 cannot force a
kill on an avoiding player 1 ($V_2$ median $+4.8\,\delta$).

**How it relates to D&S.** D&S's Appendix B has no contested category. These states are outside
both corrected zones and outside $M$, so D&S classify them as **draw**. That follows from their
convention that both players prefer a draw to a mutual kill. Under guarantees they are
**contested**: the outcome depends on whether player 2 actually takes the trade (Decision 2 says
he does) and whether player 1 then breaks off. Plan §7 anticipated this: a fat contested set "is a
finding about the formulation". Here the finding is concrete. D&S's draw zone hides a 1 % band in
which the outcome depends on preferences.

### 4.4 The inner face breaks the guarantee near $R\approx0.55$

- **Check 3.** Both failures start at $R=0.593$, the first row inside the mask on the
  $61\times62^2$ grid. Both start inside min range, closing nose-on, and leave through $R=0.2$
  within 0.5 time units.
- **Not coarseness.** On $101\times102^2$ the same two states still have
  $V^{RA}_1\approx-0.25$, about $-2\,\delta$, and they still exit.
- **Check 8.** All 6 flips more than 3 cells from the barrier are at $R=0.554$, the first coarse
  row inside the mask. There the coarse grid says $+0.18$ to $+0.27$ and the fine grid $-0.007$
  to $-0.036$.
- **What it means.** Near the inner face the value counts on play below $R=0.2$, which the domain
  does not contain. M3 found this face leaky and masked $R<0.45$. For the reach-avoid value, that
  mask is not wide enough.
- **What it does not affect.** Check 11's fine-grid starts all won. That includes 193 starts at
  $R<0.8$, 96 of them below $R=0.65$; on the fine grid the mask sits about 4 cells from the face.
  $C$ lies at $R>3$, entirely away from the face.

### 4.5 The negative control does not discriminate

- **The result.** The old heuristic, $\sigma_1=-\operatorname{sign}(\phi_1)$, also wins from every
  $W_1$ start against all five laws: 0 of 2379 starts beaten on the fine grid, 0 of 2022 on the
  coarse.
- **Not a harness fault** (§4.1). From shallow coarse starts, where $-\delta<V^{RA}_1\le0$, the
  heuristic and the grid law give the same outcomes: 383 and 383 wins against the adversary, 391
  and 392 against the trader.
- **It fits M4.** There the heuristic matched the barrier control 99 % of the time.
- **Why the plan's premise fails here.** It says the heuristic is known to be wrong, but that holds
  only at long range. $W_1$ has no state beyond $R\approx6.1$.
- **What it means.** Check 11 is a strong test of the guarantee. It is not a test that would
  separate the reach-avoid law from a good simple pursuit law.
- **The gap is now covered from the other side.** Check 13 (§4.8) tests that the boundary of
  $W_1$ is right from outside.

### 4.6 The topology check was mis-specified

- **The result.** $W_1\cap\Omega$ has 4 components: two $Rf$-mirror halves of 126,616 nodes, and
  the two off-boresight zones.
- **Why.** Excluding $T'_1$ and $R<0.45$ cuts D&S's min-range subregion in two.
- **Relax either exclusion and the count is D&S's.** Both diagnostic variants give 3: one
  $Rf$-invariant region plus the mirror pair (`diag_fields_201x204.log`).
- **Conclusion.** The structure is right. The check's definition was not.

### 4.7 Ordering and disjointness fail by small numerical margins

- **The scheme isn't strictly monotone.** WENO5 with TVD-RK3 is not, so the continuum identity
  $V^{RA}_1\ge V_1$ is violated slightly.
- **Size.** At most $0.125\,\delta$ in $\Omega$ on the coarse grid and $0.124\,\delta$ on the fine,
  where 842 of 7.2 M nodes exceed $0.01\,\delta$. Inside $T_{2\to1}$, near the corner $\eta$ at $R\approx0.79$, it reaches $0.2\,\delta$.
- **The $W_1\cap D$ nodes** (6 coarse, 16 fine) all have $|V^{RA}_1|$ and $|V_1|$ below
  $0.015\,\delta$. They are sign ties.
- **The bar.** The pre-registered $0.01\,\delta$ was tighter than this scheme can meet. The
  effect on the partition is negligible.

### 4.8 The converse guarantee, and why the contested set is a draw (added 2026-10-12)

**What check 13 tests.** It was pre-registered as criteria §7 before it ran.
- **Starts.** The states M3's old set claimed for player 1 and $W_1$ denies, one cell in from each
  edge: $V_1\le-\delta$, $V_2>0$, $V^{RA}_1\ge+\delta$. The pool is 25,402 fine-grid nodes, of
  which 2000 were drawn.
- **Where they sit.** The player-1 half of $C$: $R$ median 4.39, $|\phi_1|$ median $76°$, and
  $\phi_2$ of opposite sign to $\phi_1$, median magnitude $14°$.
- **The question.** Can player 2 always stop player 1 from winning alone?

**Result: PASS.**
- **Against the reach-avoid adversary,** none of the five player-1 laws wins alone from any start:
  0 of 10,000 runs.
- **The test is informative.** Against the merely avoiding player 2, player 1's single-target law
  wins alone from 335 starts. So these are winnable states, held only by player 2's strategy.
- **The coarse grid agrees:** 0 of 10,000, with 279 informative wins.
- **Check 11 is this test's control.** From inside $W_1$ the same adversary lost every run.

**All of player 1's deterministic laws do the same thing here.**
- The reach-avoid, single-target and time-optimal laws and the heuristic all prescribe
  $\sigma_1=-\operatorname{sign}(\phi_1)$ at every start, a hard turn toward player 2. Their kill
  times match in 1997–2000 of 2000 runs.
- Pressing is fatal. Player 2 kills player 1 in every run against the reach-avoid adversary, the
  trader and even the heuristic, with a median of 0.20 time units (90th percentile 0.45).
- Against the avoiding player 2, pressing still ends in player 2's kill in 1665 of 2000 starts.
- **Physically:** player 1's nose is $76°$ off while player 2 already points at him. Turning in
  takes player 1 into player 2's envelope before player 1's own shot comes on.

**Retreating is safe (report only, `diag_retreat_201x204.log`).** Player 1 flies the evader law
of the mirror game, $\sigma_1=+\operatorname{sign}(\partial V_2/\partial\phi_1)$.
- He is killed in **0 of 10,000** runs, against all five player-2 laws.
- Every run ends with no kill. Where the domain is left (1290 and 1910 runs against the
  adversary and the avoider), it is always through $R=12$: range opening, disengagement.

**So the player-1 half of $C$ is a draw.** Suppose player 1 retreats and player 2 plays the
reach-avoid law. Then:
- player 2 cannot kill player 1, because $V_2>0$;
- there is no mutual kill, because that needs player 1 inside $T_{2\to1}$;
- player 1 cannot win alone, because $V^{RA}_1>0$.

The outcome is a draw. Neither player can do better by switching:
- player 1 pressing dies, by check 13;
- player 2 has no way to kill a retreating player 1.

The argument needs only one preference: player 1 prefers a draw to dying alone. Player 2's
preferences don't enter, because he cannot force any outcome other than the draw. Every ordering
the project considers satisfies that (D&S's, Decision 2, and the survive-longest alternative of
Plan §11). By the index swap the player-2 half is the same.

**Only a 0.2 % sliver of $C$ stays genuinely contested**: the states where both players can
force a kill. That sliver is D&S's measure-zero $M$ (§4.2).

**What this changes.**
- D&S file these states as draws by appeal to their preference convention. The guarantee
  analysis reaches the same answer without needing that convention.
- **Caveat.** The argument uses the continuum guarantees, numerically supported here for 2000
  starts at horizon 6. The retreat runs that reach $R=12$ leave the computational domain still
  separating; reading that as a permanent disengagement is physical judgment, not a computation.
