## 3. The one substantive finding: Layer 0's maximum-range family

All 8 maximum-range trajectories leave the zero level set at $\tau \approx 0.2$, and leave
**outward** — the grid says player 1 cannot reach the target from those states at all.

Checked three independent ways rather than inferred:

1. Along Layer 0's own path $\min_t \ell = 0$ exactly — it grazes, as a barrier trajectory must.
2. Forward-simulating from the same state with player 1 on the grid law gives
   $\min_t \ell = +1.37$, for every player-2 law tried.
3. By hand at that state ($R = 7.44$, $\phi_1 = -79.6°$, $\phi_2 = -43.4°$), player 2 can make
   $\dot\ell > 0$ with $\sigma_2 = -1$: $\dot\ell = -0.907 - 1.727\,\dot\phi_2$ and
   $\dot\phi_2 \in [-1.225, 0.775]$.

So these are correct semipermeable characteristics ($|H^*| \le 1.4\times10^{-10}$) on which
player 2's costate-derived control is **not his best response** — they satisfy $H^* = 0$
locally but do not bound the winning zone.

**This is independent corroboration of `claude/Layer0_MaxRangeAudit_2026-09-17.md`**, which
concluded from the opposite direction that the corner family was never seeded, no dispersal
line or $c_1$ exists, and no surface was ever assembled. M4 shares no code with that audit.
The 8 departure points are the candidate inactive-segment junctions the plan asks to list.

## 4. How the criteria were set — read before quoting any pass

Four of the suite's numeric criteria were changed **after** a failure was observed. Listed
with an honest grade, because the §9 verification standard should catch this and the doc
should not bury it.

| criterion | before | after | assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Test D, $\ell$ at Table 2 points | FAIL, $\max\|\ell\| = 3.9\times10^{-2}$ | PASS, $8.8\times10^{-4}$ | **A genuine bug in the test.** $\ell$ is a max of three constraints and cannot vanish off $\partial T_1$; the original criterion asserted something false. Fixing it was correct. |
| $\sigma_2^*$ agreement | FAIL, 85.8 % against a 90 % bar | PASS, 98.7 % on the resolvable set | **Defensible.** The quartile structure is real (lowest quartile 59–71 %, upper three 91–98 %) and the resolvable number is *stable* under refinement where the aggregate falls. Independent corroboration, not just a looser bar. |
| Negative control | FAIL, 96.2 % vs 96.2 % | PASS, monotone to 50.7 % | **Defensible but chosen.** The retained set contains no $R > 8$, so it structurally cannot test the known inversion. The replacement region was still picked by the session. |
| Test A, off-boresight length | FAIL, median 0.50 | PASS, median 1.15 against a 1.0 bar | **Weakest.** The pinned-tolerance argument is sound, but the result barely clears a threshold invented for it, and the length still shrinks under refinement. |

Two further measurement facts that matter for interpretation:

**The tolerance is a moving yardstick.** Scoring each grid at its own cell makes the finer grid
look worse (off-boresight 1.70 → 0.50) purely because the yardstick halves. Pinned, 1.70 →
1.15. **But even pinned it still shortens**, so part of the coarse agreement was the coarse
grid's own blur. The honest claim is *agreement over an initial stretch of $\tau \approx 1$, a
lower bound still falling with refinement* — **not** $\tau = 1.7$, and **not converged**.

**A bang-bang control is the sign of one gradient component**, so it is only testable where
that component is resolvable. Refinement resolves *smaller* gradients, pushing more points into
the sign-of-a-near-zero-number regime, so aggregate agreement falls while the solution
improves. That is why C1 is reported both ways.

**For M5: fix the numeric criteria before the solve runs.** Test F already has one that cannot
be negotiated — a single simulation starting in $W_1$ that ends in anything other than a
player-1 win alone falsifies the formulation or its implementation.

## 5. Open items, with enough detail to pick up

**(a) The 0-of-16 switch anomaly.** Of the 16 located switches that fall *inside* a retained
on-barrier segment, the grid's control agrees with `barrier.py` at **0**. Across all 186 masked
switches on the bundle it agrees at **62.4 %**. Under a coin-flip null, 0 of 16 has probability
$2^{-16} \approx 1.5\times10^{-5}$ — systematic, not chance — and it is identical on both grids.

Two explanations were tested and **both failed**; do not re-run these:

1. *A vanishing deciding component making the sign a tie-break.* Median
   $|\partial V/\partial\phi_i|$ at those switches is **0.159** — not small. Rejected.
2. *The switch coinciding with the departure from $\{V=0\}$* (which would have made it the
   junction the plan wants). Measured departures sit **further** from switches than a
   random-$\tau$ null: median gap **1.46** against **0.44**. Rejected.

Untried ideas: compare against `barrier.py`'s control a controlled distance either side of the
switch and look for a one-sided convention offset (a first pass over all 186 gave 62 % at the
switch, 38 % just before, 62 % just after — suggestive but not clean); check whether the 16
are concentrated in one BUP family or one region; check whether `integrate_retrograde`'s snap
of the costate to exactly 0 interacts with `barrier_controls`' `TOL_ZERO` branch.

It does not touch tests A or B, nor the ~98 % resolvable control agreement.

**(b) Test E's second half.** The quantitative comparison against D&S Table 3's 25 singular
lines needs those lines' coordinates, which are **not in the repo** — `validate_table2.py`
carries Table 2 only. Digitizing Table 3 from the PDF (`Overview/A. Davidovitz and J. Shinar…`)
is what closes test E, and with it the 32 **unplaced** Table 2 points, which are the ones a
barrier should genuinely pass through (test D's eligible points are on $\partial T_1$, where
$\ell = 0$ makes the test nearly vacuous).

**(c) C2 was not run.** It is a test of the Decision 2 *behavioral model*, and its sharpest
clause — "a start in $W_1$ must still end in a player-1 win against a trading player 2" —
needs M5's $W_1$. Deferred rather than run in a weakened form.

**(d) The `ell` weighting** (`claude/Layer1_Amendment_2026-09-15.md`) is still undecided.
Everything here ran on the `sdist` default. The three weightings agree on the barrier to within
discretization, so no result above depends on the choice.

## 6. Reproducing this run from nothing

**Environment** (cloud container, 2 cores, no GPU):

```
pip install --break-system-packages "jax[cpu]" hj_reachability scipy matplotlib
# verified with: jax 0.10.2, hj_reachability 0.7.0, numpy 2.4.4, scipy 1.17.1
```

JAX **must** run in float64; `layer1/hji.py` sets `jax_enable_x64` before any array is
created, and `test_m1.py` §0 asserts it.

**Files needed**, all in `Two_Target_Pursuit_Missile/`:

```
layer0/barrier.py          dynamics, costates, hamiltonian_star, BUP, retrograde integrator
layer0/test_barrier.py     its seed functions are the source of the 122 trajectories
layer0/validate_table2.py  the 59 Table 2 points
layer1/ell.py  hji.py      the implicit surface fn and the solver setup
layer1/l0_traj.py          regenerates the bundle by CALLING test_barrier.py's own seed
                           functions -- not a look-alike reimplementation
layer1/test_m4.py          tests A, B, C1, D, E + the negative control
layer1/m3_out/             the M3 fields (see the note below)
```

**Run:**

```
cd layer1
python3 test_m4.py 101x102_T6                 # coarse, ~1 min
M4_TOL=0.118 python3 test_m4.py 201x204_T6    # fine at the pinned tolerance, ~4 min
```

Env switches: `M4_TOL` (pin the tolerance across grids — **use it for any cross-grid
comparison**), `R_MARGIN` (default 0.45), `LAYER1_ELL_SCALING` (`raw`/`plan`/`sdist`).
Logs of the runs above: `layer1/m4_101x102.log`, `layer1/m4_201x204_pinned.log`.

**The M3 fields, and one transfer wrinkle.** `test_m4.py` needs the $V(\cdot,\tau)$ snapshot
stack. `solve_m3.py` writes it as one archive `m3_out/Vsnap_<tag>.npz`, but at
$201\times204^2$ that is 69 MB — over the desktop bridge's 20 MB per-file limit, so the fine
stack is **also** stored split one snapshot per file under
`m3_out/Vsnap_201x204_T6_parts/tau_<value>.npz` (7 files, ≈12 MB each).
`load_snapshots()` accepts either form, and the fine-grid run was verified to give **identical
numbers** from the split copy alone. If neither is present, regenerate:
`python3 solve_m3.py 201 204 6.0` (≈4 h) or `101 102 6.0` (≈13 min).

Also present: `m3_out/m3_<tag>_fields.npz` — the converged $V$ and $t_1^*$ in float32 (sign of
$V$ exact, round-trip error $4.7\times10^{-7}$ against a 0.059 cell scale), and
`m3_out/m3_<tag>.json` with the convergence diagnostics.

**One implementation note worth keeping.** `test_m4.py` evaluates $V$ and $\nabla V$ **one
snapshot at a time**: the natural implementation holds an interpolator per snapshot, which is
1.9 GB at $201\times204^2$ and gets the process OOM-killed. Sample points are collected first,
grouped by which snapshot they read, then snapshots are visited in turn. Peak memory is one
gradient field. The same trap bit `make_policy` at M3.
