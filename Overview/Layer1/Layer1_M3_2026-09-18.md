## §10 step 2: the transpose identity, verified rather than assumed

Plan §2.5 makes `V_2 = V_1 o S` and `t_2* = t_1* o S` load-bearing: they are what stop M5
being a second overnight solve. §10 says to assert them on the grid, not in theory.

Done, and not by the cheap route. `solve_m3.py` builds the **mirror game from scratch** —
player 2 minimising, player 1 maximising, and `ell_2` written out independently from the
index asymmetry rather than by calling `ell_1` with swapped arguments — solves it, and
compares:

| grid | `max │V_2 − V_1∘S│` | winning-zone disagreement |
|---|---|---|
| 51 × 50 | 1.8e-14 | 0 nodes |
| 101 × 100 | 6.5e-13 | 0 nodes |

So M5's second field is an axis transpose.

## Converged in horizon

Marched to τ = 10 at 101 × 100 and τ = 6 elsewhere, reporting every 0.25.

**The criterion had to be fixed first.** "Has the zero level set stopped moving?" normalised
by the whole grid is meaningless — the grid is mostly empty space, so any converged surface
scores well. Normalised by the **zero level set's own node count**, the number says what the
criterion says:

| τ | motion, as a fraction of the surface itself |
|---|---|
| 0.25 | 48.8 % |
| 2.00 | 4.90 % |
| 4.00 | 1.30 % |
| 6.00 | 0.24 % |
| 10.00 | 0.038 % |

A fit of `frac(τ) = L − A e^{−kτ}` on the tail puts the infinite-horizon winning zone at
**12.0236 %**; an independent march to τ = 10 landed at **12.0271 %** — so the extrapolation
from τ ≤ 6 data predicts the τ = 10 result to 0.004 % of grid volume. Outer edge in R is
**6.100** on every grid, inside `R_hi(0) = 6.1416`: D&S's bounded winning zone.

## Converged in grid

Two nested pairs, both at τ = 6. **The grids nest on purpose** — n_R 101 → 201 (endpoints
included) and n_φ n → 2n (endpoint=False) make every coarse node a fine node, so the
comparison is exact subsampling `V_fine[::2,::2,::2]`. A 101/201 pair in the periodic angles
would *not* nest (2π/101 against 2π/201) and the study would be measuring its own interpolant
as much as the solver.

**The criterion is displacement, not a node count.** A count of flipped nodes grows with the
surface's area whatever the solver does. Spreading the symmetric-difference volume over the
surface area gives a thickness.

## The one defect: `n_φ` a multiple of 8 puts grid nodes on |φ₁| = β

β = π/4 lands exactly on a node whenever n_φ is a multiple of 8 (the node index is
0.625 n_φ). That is the target set's own kink — where `ell`'s max switches between the
boresight term and the range terms.

Reflection invariance is **exact in the continuum**: `ell` is Rf-invariant to 4e-15 and the
dynamics are Rf-equivariant with the controls negated, so `V(R, −φ₁, −φ₂) = V(R, φ₁, φ₂)`
identically. Any residual is numerical error — which makes it a free and very sharp check on
an expensive computation.

| grid | β on a node? | `max │V − V∘Rf│` | `t_1*` mirror mismatch | M5 smoke test (raw) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 101 × 100 | no | 3.6e-09 | 0 nodes | 100.0000 % |
| 201 × 200 | **yes** | 9.9e-04 | **21 642 nodes** (2.2 % of the zone) | 98.8407 % |
| 101 × 102 | no | 4.5e-10 | 0 nodes | 100.0000 % |
| 201 × 204 | no | 2.8e-04 | **0 nodes** | **100.0000 %** |

The error grows with τ (6e-8 at τ=2, 5e-7 at τ=3, 9.9e-4 at τ=6) and with resolution. An
isolated experiment at n_R = 31 with n_φ = 104 (β on-node) against 102 (off-node) shows the
on-node grid 55× worse at τ = 6 — confirming the mechanism while showing it needs the fine R
grid and the long horizon to reach 1e-3.

**Why it mattered.** M3's own criteria are about the barrier, where the residual is 1.7 % of
one cell and irrelevant. But M5's smoke test *is* this symmetry: on |φ₁| = |φ₂| the identity
`t_1* = t_2*` reduces exactly to Rf-invariance of `t_1*`, because `S x = Rf x` there. And
M5's real question is whether anything **besides** that diagonal appears — so a 2.2 % symmetry
error in `t_1*` is noise injected straight into the answer.

**Why it was not patched.** Projecting onto the symmetry (`min(t, t∘Rf)`) restores the smoke
test to exactly 100 % and the residual to 0. That is a legitimate operation — it removes
numerical error from an exact symmetry — but it is the wrong thing to measure M5 on, because
projection could equally hide a genuine open-volume component or manufacture one.
`grid_study.py` reports the smoke test raw *and* projected, never only projected.

**The fix is a grid choice.** `n_φ = 102 → 204` keeps β off-node (indices 63.75 and 127.5)
*and* still nests, since 204 = 2 × 102. That pair was solved and is green on every check with
no projection. Note the residual in `V` itself is 2.8e-04 even off-node — a shock-ridge effect
at φ₁ ≈ ±π that grows with resolution. It passes the "< 1 % of a cell" bar, but it is the
thing to watch if M4's test B (gradient direction) behaves oddly near the ridge.

## Two structural findings

### The single-target winning zone is connected

D&S report five disconnected closed subregions. The converged `{V ≤ 0}` is **one** component
(99.96 % of the zone) at both resolutions, and connected in every R section too.

**This is not a discrepancy.** Layer 1 solves the *uncorrected single-target* reach problem —
the same one Layer 0's barriers are seeded from, which Plan §6 records as "exactly right as
ground truth for this layer". D&S's five subregions are the *corrected* zone,
`T_1' = T_1 \ closure(T_2)` (Appendix B). Different objects; the disconnection is a candidate
consequence of the correction, and testing that is Layer 2's.

### The set {V₁ ≤ 0} \ {V₂ ≤ 0} has three components, not five

*(Retitled 2026-10-01; this was "the win-alone set". It is not a win-alone set: player 1's reach
strategy does not steer around $T_{2\to1}$, and "player 2 cannot force $T_{2\to1}$ against an
avoiding player 1" says nothing about a player 1 who is not avoiding. The true win-alone set is
M5's reach-avoid set $W_1$. The name `W_1` below is kept as it appears in `win_alone.py`.)*

The nearest object Layer 1 could form without a second solve is
`W_1 = {V_1 ≤ 0} \ {V_2 ≤ 0}` — "player 1 can guarantee a kill and player 2 cannot" — free via
the transpose. Indicative only: Appendix B corrects the *target set before solving*, so
removing a set afterwards is not the same computation.

`W_1` is **7.17 %** of the grid on the fine clean grid (7.05 % coarse), in **3** components:
91.0 %, 4.46 %, 4.46 %, the latter two exact Rf mirrors of each other. Stable under refinement.

**It briefly counted 5, and that is the part worth recording.** Two of those five were 23-node
blobs at the inner R face with |V| < 0.015 — about 12 % of one cell — which would have made the
count agree with D&S exactly. Forward-simulating from them under the grid's own policy gives
`min ell = +0.41` to `+0.54`: they are not reachable states. They are leakage from the
extrapolation condition at the singular R → 0 face (Plan §3, trap 2), they sit at R = 0.200 and
0.318, and they account for the only grid-study disagreements not adjacent to the barrier. An
inner-boundary mask `R ≥ 0.45` removes them, and the count is then a stable **3** from R ≥ 0.45
through R ≥ 0.80.

**Carry into M4:** the same mask belongs in tests A–E, and `{V ≤ 0}` should never be quoted
without it.

### One number M5 will want

`{V_1 ≤ 0} ∩ {V_2 ≤ 0}` — both players *can* force a kill — is **4.53 %** of the grid, stable
between 101×100 (4.5185 %) and 201×204 (4.5276 %), so it is a genuine open volume rather than
resolution noise, and only 9 % of it lies within one cell of |φ₁| = |φ₂|.

**This is not the mutual-kill set and must not be reported as one.** It is the set where each
player has a guarantee *against a purely avoiding opponent*, and both guarantees cannot be
realised at once — exactly Plan §2.3's ambiguous-band caveat. *(2026-10-01: the next two sentences are superseded — M5 is now the reach-avoid partition, and
comparing arrival times is withdrawn.)* M5's question is about
`{t_1* = t_2*}`, a comparison of arrival times, which is a different and smaller object. The
4.53 % is recorded here as the upper bound the band lives inside. *(It remains useful as a
comparison figure for M5's contested set, but it is not an upper bound on it: the two are
defined in different games.)*
